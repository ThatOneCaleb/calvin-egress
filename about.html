About the project

Built to make building safety something you can see.

Evacuation planning usually lives in spreadsheets and code tables. Calvin Egress turns it into something you can watch — a building, a fire, and the people trying to get out — so the trade-offs behind a safety decision are legible to anyone in the room.

Why it exists

Calvin Egress began as an agent-based model for a Calvin University safety-planning research project. The goal was a tool that could answer the questions a campus actually faces — Does voice evacuation get people out faster than a bell? Do these sprinklers buy enough time? What happens if that stairwell is blocked? — and show the answer, floor by floor, in real time.

It is deliberately performance-based: instead of only checking boxes against a code, it simulates the physics of the egress and compares the time people need to get out (RSET) against the time before conditions turn deadly (ASET). That comparison is the heart of modern fire-safety engineering.

Who it's for

  • Safety planners & administrators weighing concrete interventions against each other.
  • Researchers & students exploring how crowd behavior, building geometry, and fire interact.
  • Anyone in the meeting who needs to understand a recommendation without reading the code behind it.

Design principles

Grounded, not guessed

Every number traces to pedestrian science or fire-safety engineering — Weidmann/Fruin walking speeds, SFPE pre-movement, FED tenability. See the methodology.

Honest about its edges

The model states exactly where it is and isn't trustworthy. It's a comparative planning tool, not certified CFD — and the validation page says so plainly.

Open in one click

The whole simulator runs in a browser with no install and no account, so a result can be reproduced live in front of the people who need to see it.

Who built it

Calvin Egress is designed and built by Caleb Steele, within a Calvin University research context, for use in campus safety planning. The engine, geometry pipeline, and this site are developed as a single evolving project, with a roadmap toward research-grade validation.

Status

Version 1.0 — a working, sanity-checked browser engine. The roadmap covers RiMEA verification and the path to swappable research-grade backends.
