Methodology

The model, in full — and exactly how far it goes.

Calvin Egress is a performance-based egress + fire/smoke tenability model. It computes ASET vs RSET — the available versus required safe egress time — the central comparison of fire-safety engineering.

Geometry

The world is a 1-metre grid, one cell per square metre. Hekman Library is a real digitized interior across four floors; every other Calvin building is a procedural interior generated to fit its true OpenStreetMap footprint polygon. Rooms and doorways are inferred from the wall grid — rooms as connected components of navigable space, doors as the one-cell pinch-points between them — so fire and people must channel through openings rather than drift through walls.

Routing

Occupants navigate a static floor field: a multi-source breadth-first search outward from every stairwell and exit assigns each cell its geodesic distance to safety. People descend that gradient toward the nearest egress. Stairwells connect floors as capacity-limited bottlenecks, so upper-floor crowds queue and merge exactly where real buildings jam.

Movement & speeds

The clock is real: one simulation tick is 0.25 s. Free-flow walking speed is set by demographic group, then reduced by local crowd density following a Weidmann-style fundamental diagram — the denser the crowd, the slower everyone moves.

Speeds follow Weidmann/Fruin pedestrian data. Each person also carries individual variation and a distinct body size, so the crowd is genuinely heterogeneous — visible on screen as differently colored, differently sized occupants.

Behavior

Each occupant has an OCEAN personality that drives how they act under stress: when they start moving (pre-movement), how hard they push in a crowd (which can cause crush falls), whether they stop to help, and how familiar they are with the building's exits. Lower cooperation produces more competitive pushing and more crush casualties at bottlenecks.

The RSET timeline

Required Safe Egress Time is modeled explicitly as a sequence, so every lever moves the correct term:

  • Detection → alarm. Detector coverage and alarm type set when the evacuation clock starts.
  • Pre-movement. A lognormal delay about an adjustable median (SFPE) — voice evacuation shortens it versus a bell or horn.
  • Travel. Density-dependent walking, queueing, and stair descent to the exits.

Fire

Fire follows a t²-style growth ramp and spreads cell-to-cell — fast across open space, throttled at doorways according to door policy (propped open, self-closing, or auto-close / fire-rated). Sprinklers, when present, cap the growth, which materially extends the available time. Fire climbs between floors through stairwells like a chimney.

Smoke & tenability

Smoke is modeled as its own front — it outruns the flame, fills rooms, and travels corridors and stairwells. It does two things to people: it reduces walking speed and causes wrong turns as visibility drops (Jin), and it accrues a fractional effective dose (FED). Only smoke above a concentration threshold adds to the dose; when the cumulative dose crosses the incapacitation limit, that occupant is lost. The time until conditions become untenable is ASET — the number RSET must beat.

Planning levers

The things a university actually controls are all adjustable: alarm type, detection delay, sprinklers, door policy, locked or blocked exits, occupant load, demographic mix, and cooperation. A second tier of intervention and human-factor sweeps (floor wardens, drills, signage, phone distraction, bulky attire, background noise, herding, and more) lets you test whether an "obvious" factor actually changes the outcome — each has a real pathway into the model.

Calibration values

  • 1 cell = 1 m; 1 tick = 0.25 s; density → speed approximates Weidmann.
  • Pre-movement lognormal about an adjustable median; voice-evacuation factor ≈ 0.62 (SFPE).
  • FED: only smoke above a threshold accrues dose; incapacitation at a cumulative dose.
  • Door flame factor — open 0.6 / self-closing 0.10 / auto-close or rated 0 (compartmentation); smoke similarly.

Run modes

A single live run plays one scenario in 3D. Monte-Carlo runs the same scenario many times and reports the distribution of RSET, deaths, and the percentage of runs in which everyone escapes — the defensible way to read a stochastic model.
