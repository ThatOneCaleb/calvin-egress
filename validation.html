Validation & limitations

A tool is only useful if you know where to trust it.

Calvin Egress passes a battery of behavioral sanity checks and is honest about the rest. Here's what's been verified, what isn't yet, and the path to research-grade.

01  Verified behavior

Sanity checks that pass

Automated engine tests confirm the model moves in the right direction for the levers that matter — the minimum bar before any result is worth reading.

Speed hierarchy matches literature

Able adults > older adults > children > mobility-impaired, in line with Weidmann/Fruin free-flow data.

Sprinklers save lives

Turning sprinklers on cuts mean deaths from ≈ 66 to ≈ 9 in a representative Hekman fire by capping fire growth and extending ASET.

Slower detection costs lives

Delaying detection and alarm raises casualties (≈ 60 → ≈ 82), because every second is added to the front of the RSET timeline.

Capacity & cooperation matter

Narrower or locked exits and lower crowd cooperation worsen clearance time and crush casualties, as expected at bottlenecks.

Runs always terminate

A stall-guard ends every run and flags genuinely trapped occupants, so results are never an artifact of a deadlock.

Monte-Carlo returns a distribution

Repeated runs produce a spread of outcomes — not a single fragile number — so conclusions rest on the distribution.

Known limitations

Stated plainly, because a planning tool is only defensible if its boundaries are explicit.

  • Not CFD fire. Fire is a t²-style cellular spread with FED tenability, not a NIST FDS computation. It captures growth, compartmentation, and tenability trends — not detailed combustion physics.
  • Door ratings are an assumption. No real door schedules exist for the buildings, so fire-door behavior is an adjustable modeling assumption rather than measured data.
  • Door channeling is geometry-limited. The coarse 1 m Hekman digitization merged many rooms, so few one-cell doorways are detected and door policy has a muted effect there. The effect is clear on buildings with proper room walls; finer floor plans would fix it.
  • Simplified crowd physics. A cell-capacity model, not continuous body-force clogging — it does not reproduce the full "faster-is-slower" arching at doorways.
  • Generated interiors approximate reality. Only Hekman reflects real plans; other Calvin buildings are plausible interiors fit to a real footprint.

What this means for use

Treat results as comparative: "which scenario clears faster / loses fewer people?" is defensible; a precise predicted death count for a specific real fire is not. The strongest conclusions come from relative comparisons and Monte-Carlo distributions, not single absolute numbers.

Path to research-grade

The browser engine is the product; the credibility work ahead is well-defined:

  • RiMEA test suite. Implement the standard verification cases — Test 4 (fundamental diagram), Test 6 (corner), Test 7 (speed distribution), Test 11 (route choice) — and confirm outputs fall within accepted tolerances.
  • Measured door flow. Confirm specific flow lands in the ≈ 1.0–1.4 persons/m/s range reported in the literature.
  • Smoke / FED calibration. Tie the tenability model to published fire-engineering data.
  • Swappable research backends. Expose the same scenario interface to validated engines — JuPedSim or Vadere for crowd dynamics, FDS or CFAST for certified fire — feeding the same 3D front-end for eventual certification.
